How to Use the Canadian Tax Calculator

The Canadian Tax Calculator computes federal and provincial income tax, CPP (Canada Pension Plan) contributions, and EI (Employment Insurance) premiums for all 13 provinces and territories for the 2026 tax year. It shows your after-tax take-home pay by pay period.

Enter your gross income, province, filing status, and any RRSP contributions. The calculator applies the correct 2026 federal and provincial tax brackets, CPP rates, EI premium rates, and the basic personal amount to show your total deductions and net pay.

A key nuance is that provincial tax rates vary dramatically: for the same $80,000 income, a worker in Quebec (combined rate ~37%) pays significantly more than someone in Alberta (~30%) or Ontario (~32%). Provincial differences in healthcare premiums, surtaxes, and personal amount credits also affect take-home pay.

📊 Worked Example

$80,000 gross income in Ontario, 2026:

  • Federal income tax: $12,516
  • Ontario provincial tax: $5,198
  • CPP contributions: $3,867
  • EI premiums: $1,049
  • Take-home: $57,370 (~72%)

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Canadian federal income tax brackets for 2026?

Federal tax rates (projected 2026 with inflation adjustments): 15% on the first ~$57,375, 20.5% on ~$57,376–$114,750, 26% on ~$114,751–$158,519, 29% on ~$158,520–$220,000, and 33% on income above $220,000. These are marginal rates — only the income in each bracket is taxed at that rate.

What is the CPP contribution for 2026?

CPP (Canada Pension Plan) contributions in 2025 are 5.95% of earnings between the $3,500 basic exemption and the $68,500 maximum pensionable earnings (Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings). Additional CPP2 contributions apply at 4% on earnings between the first and second YMPE. 2026 figures will be updated based on official CRA announcements.

Which Canadian province has the highest income tax?

Quebec consistently has the highest combined federal+provincial income tax rate for most income levels. Nova Scotia also has high rates. Alberta has no provincial sales tax and comparatively lower income tax. The combination of federal + provincial tax makes the effective rate complex to compare without a calculator accounting for all credits and deductions.

How does an RRSP contribution reduce my tax?

RRSP contributions are deducted from your taxable income, reducing your tax bill immediately. A $10,000 RRSP contribution from a taxpayer in the 33% combined federal+provincial rate saves approximately $3,300 in income tax that year. The money grows tax-deferred inside the RRSP and is taxed at withdrawal (ideally in lower-income retirement years).

What is the difference between RRSP and TFSA?

RRSP: contributions are tax-deductible (reducing income now), growth is tax-deferred, withdrawals are taxed as income. Best for those in a high tax bracket now expecting lower income in retirement. TFSA: contributions are from after-tax income (no immediate deduction), but all growth and withdrawals are completely tax-free. Best for those expecting similar or higher income in retirement or for flexible emergency savings.
